This paper presents the first neural embedding system capable of matching geographic place names across 20 writing systems without requiring language identification or phonetic resources at inference time. No existing method can determine that Latin "Beijing", Chinese "北京", Arabic "بکن", and Cyrillic "Пекин" refer to the same location using only raw character input. The system achieves state-of-the-art performance on the only available cross-script toponym benchmark (MEHDIE), and is deployed at production scale over 67 million toponyms with sub-second retrieval — demonstrating operational readiness for geospatial data integration rather than a research prototype. An ablation study confirms that the neural training curriculum doubles performance over the raw phonetic features it learns from, establishing that learned alignment is essential for cross-script matching. The approach directly addresses a core barrier to integrating multilingual geographic databases, which is central to the Digital Earth vision that this journal serves.
