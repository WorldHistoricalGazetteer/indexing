About Team Yeokji (역지사지, 歷地思之)
- Established in 2023, Team Yeokji (pronounced: yolk-gee) is a Korean research group dedicated to the study of Historical GIS.
- The team’s full name, “Yeokjisaji,” was originally devised a double entendre: one for “yeok-ji-sa-ji” (易地思之; “to put oneself in another’s shoes” in Korean), and the other for “yeok-sa-ji-ri” (歷史地理; “historical geography”). For brevity’s sake, however, the group finally selected “Team Yeokji” as its official English name.
- Team Yeokji actively shares research achievements and GIS layers on historical geography through its wiki-based platform: https://www.hisgeo.info. In the long run, it aims to develop a full-scale Web GIS service.

Database #1: Place names and map symbols printed on Daedongyeojido
- Daedongyeojido (大東輿地圖; Territorial Map of the Great East) is a woodblock-printed map that covers the entire territory of Joseon in the late 19th century. Not only does it represent the pinnacle of Joseon cartography, but it also serves as the most important resource when studying pre-modern historical geography in Korea.
- Team Yeokji converted all the place names and map symbols from the paper map to digital files. The task started as groundwork for a complete, unified dataset that would encompass all place names mentioned in significant historical resources. The result amounts to 11,229 place names and 4,226 map symbols (15,529 unique data points after removing overlaps).
- For an overview of Daedongyeojido in English alongside its images:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daedongyeojido
  - https://www.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0402000000.do?searchId=search&schM=view&relicId=4502

Database #2: County seats and administrative districts during the Joseon Dynasty
- Team Yeokji has restored the governing entities in two formats, point and polygon, given that a point alone was insufficient to understand or analyze the spatial structure of pre-modern Korea.
- Two critical moments resulted in extensive changes to the districts: the Japanese colonial redistricting order in 1914 and the north-south division in 1945. Still, scholars recognize all the minor changes in between those major events. To address the issue, Team Yeokji is now constructing a continuous, year-by-year database of administrative districts and county seats (邑治, eupchi) spanning the entire Joseon Dynasty (1392–1910).
- The database structure is designed similarly to the CHGIS (China Historical GIS).
- Spatial layers upon a continuous time series can be challenging for general users to navigate through. Thus, Team Yeokji also provides map layers centered on specific years—key moments, snapshots, or highlights in history—for better accessibility.
