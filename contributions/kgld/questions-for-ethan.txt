The tool is called Map your Data. It runs entirely in your browser: you load your own spreadsheet, it proposes matches against the eleven-million-odd places we already hold, and you accept, reject or adjust them yourself. It also lets you assign Getty AAT place types from a searchable hierarchy, and — the part that matters most for lakes — it lets you place each record on a map, either by drawing a point, line or polygon yourself, or by adopting the geometry of a record you have matched to. When you are satisfied, a single button builds a Linked Places file and submits it to us. Nothing leaves your browser until you press it.

The reason I am suggesting it is your coordinates, or rather their absence. Sixty-five of your seventy-three entities have no latitude or longitude, and we understand from kyrgyzstan_lakes_gis.md and methodology section 12 exactly why: a coordinate enters your layer only when a registered source supports it, and you have declined to guess. That is the right instinct and we are not asking you to relax it. But a gazetteer is fundamentally a spatial index, so a record without a location is one we can store and search by name and yet cannot place on a map, cannot return from any spatial query, and cannot match against the several hundred Kyrgyz lakes we already hold. In practice it becomes findable only by someone who already knows the name. Our tool will not let a record be submitted without a location, which sounds like an obstacle and is really the point: it puts the decision in the hands of the person best placed to make it, with the existing gazetteer records visible alongside for comparison.

Where you adopt an existing record's shape — an OpenStreetMap lake polygon, say — the file records that the geometry came from that record rather than from you, and marks it as less certain. Where you draw one yourself, it records that you drew it. Neither is presented as a surveyed position. I mention this because it is the kind of distinction your methodology already insists on, and we would not want your work arriving in our index looking more precise than you intended.

On dates, your dataset has already improved our tooling, and I would like to tell you how because it bears on what we are asking for. Linked Places wants a temporal statement on every record, and until last week our tool offered only three routes to one: a date column, a dataset-wide year range, or a named historical period. For seventy-three lakes all three would have been an invention — a lake is not an event and has no date. The honest formulation, which the format does allow, is that the lake is undated but the polygon is not: it was derived from a particular image on a particular date. That is precisely what your own GIS note asks of any polygon product, and reading it is what prompted the change. Our tool now has a "Geometry captured (date)" field for exactly this, either per row or as a range for the whole dataset, and it is kept deliberately separate from the dates used to find matching records — a capture date should not influence which places we think yours are.

So the practical request is: where you supply or draw a geometry, if you can record when it was captured and from what, that is all we need to make the record valid without attaching a date to the lake itself. Where you instead adopt an existing record's shape, you owe nothing — the file keeps that record's own provenance, since a capture date would say nothing about a shape someone else made. I should add that this feature is new and you would be the first person to use it in earnest, so if it does not fit the way your sources actually describe their imagery, that is useful for us to hear rather than something to work around.

The nine study-local supraglacial features on the Southern Inylchek are a harder question, and we would rather put it to you than decide it quietly. Our first instinct was to leave them out because your identity_status field says they are not to be promoted to permanent geographic entities. On reflection that is a physical-geography reason rather than a gazetteer one: a historical gazetteer exists precisely to record things that were there and then were not, and impermanence is an argument for inclusion rather than against it. We already carry five of your short-lived lakes on exactly that basis, and four of those have no coordinates either, so neither impermanence nor missing geometry really separates the nine from the five.

What does give us pause is naming. "Southern Inylchek supraglacial Lake 1" is your source's figure numbering rather than a name anyone uses, and a different study would number them differently, so a reader searching for that string would never find it and nothing else could ever match it. That, we think, is what your study_local_feature status is really recording, and it is a fair objection where impermanence was not.

Set against that, excluding them costs more than nine records. Six of your dated drainage events and nine of your observations hang off those features and nothing else, including the two simultaneous multi-lake drainages of May and July 2018 — which, as far as we can tell, are the only day-precision multi-entity events anywhere in the dataset and among the most distinctive things in it. Lake 1 alone drains and recharges four times across two seasons.

We have decided to include them, and to solve the naming problem by naming them, since a name is in the end simply what people call something and these had nobody to call them anything. In the file we are sending you they appear as "Hamilton 1", "Hamilton 3" and so on, after you, with the numeral taken from your own study-local lake numbering so the thread back to Sakurai et al.'s figures is not lost. We hope you will take that as it is meant.

Because the names are ours and not anyone else's, each one carries a citation saying so in as many words: coined by the World Historical Gazetteer for this contribution, after the compiler of the dataset, and not a local, historical or otherwise attested toponym. Your source's own label is kept alongside as a second name, cited to the paper and its figure numbering. A gazetteer may coin a name when nothing else exists, but it should never let a coined name pass as one people actually use, and we would rather the record said that plainly than have a future reader assume otherwise. If you would prefer a different convention, or would rather they were not named after you, please say so and we will change it.
