† = characters only on six-inch maps published before 1879 (per the sheet's own footnote)
‡ = “on the more recent maps” (added categories)
* = size varies with extent — ONLY Bogs/Moors/Forests + Hills; all others fixed
GB1900 (EDITION 2, 16,450 sheets): 99.7% published ≥1879 (95.6% ≥1897); only 0.3% pre-1879. Plan: build the ≥1879 classifier first, then adapt a <1879 classifier from it (the † letterforms genuinely differ).

Font-style taxonomy (training classes)

Exemplar (Characteristic Sheet)Style class → OS meaningOS categories coveredVerify
Administrative — serif CAPS caps
Size encodes administrative rank (fixed per rank). The single letter (C/H/R/L/P/T/B/W/D/E) is the boundary MARK on the line, not the label font — the label font is caps. Level is resolved by SIZE + TEXT, not font. Many are † (pre-1879) or ‡ (recent maps only).
 County Names, Hundreds†, Divisions of Counties/Ridings†, Poor Law Unions, Urban Sanitary Districts, Liberties, Parishes (Mother/Ancient)†, Civil Parishes/Townships, Divisions & Subdivisions of Townships†, Boroughs (Parl./Munic.), Cities returning Members†/not†, Wards, Market Towns†, Other Towns†, Towns generally, Town Districts‡, Parliamentary Div. of Counties‡, County Boroughs‡, Extra Parochial†, Turnpike Trusts†
Settlement / building — roman UPRIGHT upright
The default place-name face. Upright vs italic is only ~3° of slant — NOT reliably separable from font alone (validated ~0.71 ceiling); disambiguated by word-semantics + size.
 Parish Churches & Villages, Workhouses, Isolated Houses, County/Trust Bridges, Towns generally
Minor settlement / industry — roman ITALIC upright
Italic distinguishes minor/subordinate places from principal ones. Mild slant; see caveat above.
 Chapelries & Other Churches†, Other Villages, Gentlemens Seats, Manufactories/Mines/Farms/Locks
Parks & Demesnes — bold CAPS caps
Bold caps; visually near the admin caps class.
 Parks and Demesnes
Water — ITALIC italic
A PRONOUNCED cursive italic — much more slanted than settlement italic, hence the one serif class that re-grounds to usable separability (0.72 vs 0.14). Railway-Mineral shares this italic → split by TEXT. Bays & Harbours are UPRIGHT caps, not italic.
 Navigable Rivers & Canals (italic caps), Small Rivers & Brooks (italic l/c)
Bogs / Moors / Forests — CAPS, size-variable* caps
* SIZE varies with extent/importance (one of only TWO size-variable groups). Subordinate parts engraved in Roman print or stump character per extent.
 Bogs, Moors and Forests†
Ranges of Hills — size-variable* upright
* The other size-variable group. All OTHER categories have FIXED canonical size → size is a reliable discriminator everywhere except here and Bogs/Moors/Forests.
 Ranges of Hills (separate parts / single features)
crop pending Woods & Copses upright
Capture crop pending.
 Woods and Copses
Antiquities: ROMAN — serif caps caps
Roman antiquities and road names share this serif-caps face → font alone can't separate them; text/context must.
 Antiquities (Roman) — SAME FACE AS ROAD NAMES
Antiquities: PRE-HISTORIC or SAXON — blackletter blackletter
Gothic/blackletter. Hard to separate from the Norman blackletter (the middle pair).
 Antiquities (Pre-historic or Saxon)
Antiquities: NORMAN — blackletter (Old English) blackletter
A heavier Old-English blackletter. Middle pair with Saxon — expect confusion.
 Antiquities (Norman)
Antiquities: SUBSEQUENT — italic italic
Fourth antiquity font (italic serif). Distinct from the two blackletters and from Roman caps.
 Antiquities (Subsequent)
Railways (Passenger) + Stations — upright upright
Upright.
 Railways (Passenger), Principal Stations, Other Stations
Railways (Mineral) — italic italic
Italic — shares the water italic face; split from water by TEXT.
 Railways (Mineral)
Contour altitudes / spot heights — NUMERAL numeral
From the c.1923 sheet. Handled well by the text typer (pure digits).
 Contour altitudes (…200), spot heights (7·)

From the c.1923 sheet. Feeds the abbreviation lexicon (B.M.).
